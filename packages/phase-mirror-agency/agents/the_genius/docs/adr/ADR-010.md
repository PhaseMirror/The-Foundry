## 1. Context
Z-MOD requires a formal mechanism to embed the optimizer state—consisting of continuous neural network parameters $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and a discrete prime-multiplicity vector $\pi \in \{0, 1\}^m$—into the complex plane $\mathbb{C}$. This embedding allows the application of complex-analytic potentials, specifically those derived from the Riemann zeta function, to guide the optimization process.

## 2. Decision
We define the canonical complex embedding $s(\theta, \pi)$ as:

$$s(\theta, \pi) = \sigma_0 + i \left[ a \tilde{\phi}(\theta) + \sum_{p \in P} \pi_p \log p \right]$$

Where:
- $\sigma_0 \in (0, 1)$ is a fixed real part (typically 0.5 or 0.6).
- $a > 0$ is a scaling factor for the continuous energy.
- $P = \{p_1, p_2, \dots, p_m\}$ is a fixed set of small primes (e.g., $\{2, 3, 5\}$).
- $\pi_p \in \{0, 1\}$ are the components of the multiplicity vector.
- $\tilde{\phi}(\theta)$ is the **clipped parameter-energy functional**:
  $$\tilde{\phi}(\theta) = \min(\phi(\theta), \Phi_{max})$$
  with $\phi(\theta) = \frac{1}{n} \|\theta\|^2$ and $\Phi_{max}$ as a stability threshold.

## 3. Alternatives Considered
- **Unclipped Energy:** Rejected due to risks of numerical instability and "runaway" imaginary parts when $\theta$ grows large.
- **Direct Parameter Mapping:** Mapping each $\theta_j$ to a separate complex point was considered but rejected in favor of a global energy-based scalar summary $t(\theta, \pi) = \text{Im } s$, which significantly reduces the computational overhead of evaluating $\zeta(s)$.

## 4. Dependencies
- ADR-000: Project Scope and Falsifiability
- Riemann Zeta function numerical implementation

## 5. Risks
- **Information Loss:** Collapsing $\mathbb{R}^n$ into a scalar energy $\phi(\theta)$ loses coordinate-specific information. This is mitigated by the fact that the zeta potential acts as a global regularizer/modulator rather than the primary descent direction.
- **Singularity at $s=1$:** Since $\sigma_0 < 1$, the embedding avoids the pole at $s=1$.

## 6. Consequences
- **Positive:** Provides a stable, low-dimensional bridge between high-dimensional weights and zeta-analytic structures.
- **Negative:** Requires careful tuning of $a$ and $\Phi_{max}$ to ensure the imaginary part covers relevant regions of the critical strip.
